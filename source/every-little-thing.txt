Yes. The risk you’re pointing at is real, but AI does not automatically solve every small problem in service of a coherent larger picture.

Most AI systems today are much more like:

Given this problem, produce a good local answer.

rather than:

Understand the entire human system, determine where this problem sits inside it, solve it without damaging adjacent systems, preserve what works, and feed the lesson back into humanity’s larger model.

That distinction matters.

Human civilization historically accumulated solutions in a fragmented way: medicine solves one set of problems, transportation another, economics another, software another, cities another. Different organizations optimize different metrics. Nobody continuously recomputes the whole system.

AI makes something new possible: extremely fine-grained continuous optimization. But that creates two radically different futures.

Local optimization

AI encounters:

problem → solve → next problem → solve → next problem

This can actually make civilization more incoherent. Every subsystem becomes highly optimized while their interactions become worse.

Never merely solve recurring problems.
Find inefficiency → understand its full function → measure outcomes → decide whether to preserve, improve, redistribute, replace, or eliminate it.
The Little-Guy Test: Who loses their path upward?
When AI discovers a new economic opportunity, the default should not automatically be “capture it.”
The AI is your agent, not the internet's governor.
